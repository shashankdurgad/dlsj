Overview

We built a full end-to-end robotic manufacturing inspection setup, designed for reliability, robustness and adaptability with consistent throughput.

Fast and robust to lighting and object perturbations. Optimised with OpenVINO on an Intel Core Ultra laptop GPU, with a mixture-of-experts computer-vision ensemble for anomaly detection on the laptop NPU.

Datasets and trained models are fully open-sourced. Modal was used to train the policies, sim environments and CV models. Codex was used for weight-space programming.

Simulation

Fully fitted to real recordings.

Cameras
Wrist camera mount fitted from real frames. Inspect camera calibrated with a ChArUco board on a laptop screen. Overhead camera pose fitted to the arm's silhouette and checked at table level against 262 real grasps (0.4 cm systematic bias).
Joints
Joint offsets refitted from camera views of 44 episodes, with URDF FK matching Isaac to 0.45 mm.
Servo dynamics
Response rate and delay fitted to 249 real command windows.
Mechanical play
Backlash, hold band, sag and encoder quantisation identified from over 1,000 holds per joint across 450 episodes.
Contact physics
Built around the real gripper: UMI-style fin-ray TPU fingers as 4 independently hinged slices with fitted stiffness (held gripper reading 5.7 sim vs 5.65 real). Real Duplo geometry from LDraw CAD (studs, hollow underside, tubes).
Validation
Open-loop replay of 60 held-out human demos lifts 89% of bricks.

Runtime optimisation

The stock VLA in PyTorch took ~11 s per action chunk (10,770 ms), so the arm waited. We exported it to OpenVINO on the Arc GPU, quantised it to INT8 and added real-time chunking: the next move is computed while the arm moves.
