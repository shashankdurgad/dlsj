# Fix the gripper before training longer

Practitioners post-train π0.5 with plain supervised flow-matching. The usual recipe is a full fine-tune from `pi05_base`: AdamW with β=(0.9, 0.95) and gradient clip 1.0, peak LR 2.5e-5 to 5e-5 after warmup, 20k–30k steps at batch 32–256, quantile normalization, an action chunk of 10–50, and checkpoints chosen by real-robot rollouts rather than loss. Our run was about 2,300 updates, expert-only, with a frozen backbone, so it sits well below every published recipe. At a batch of 32 that is about 1.6 passes over the 47k frames, against roughly 6k steps for the smallest SO-101 expert-only recipe known to work and 20k–30k for the official ones. Training longer is necessary, but it does not explain the failure. Both ACT and π0.5 find the brick and never close the gripper, and two architectures failing the same way points at a shared cause. The likely suspects are the gripper channel in the data (encoding, range, closure timing, idle frames) or the deployment path (normalization stats, unit conversion, open-loop chunk length, INT8 quantization of a sparse dimension). The Isaac Sim result says little, because the scene is approximate and published sim twins overestimate real success. For the next 24–36 hours we recommend this order. First, spend about two hours on gripper and plumbing forensics. Second, clean the data. Third, run a full fine-tune with an unfrozen backbone for about 15k steps on one 80 GB GPU (roughly 8–16 GPU-hours). Fourth, deploy with 10–25 executed steps per chunk or RTC, and keep FP16 as a fallback to INT8 for the action expert. Fifth, if time remains, one round of leader-arm corrections (HG-DAgger, optionally RECAP-labelled). Online RL is not worth it at hackathon scale.

## Official recipes train the whole model for 20k–30k steps

openpi ships six real π0.5 training configs. Every one of them is a full fine-tune, and none uses LoRA or an expert-only setup. They share AdamW (b1 0.9, b2 0.95, weight decay 1e-10, gradient clip 1.0), bf16 mixed precision, 224×224 images, 32-dim padded actions, `max_token_len` 200, and 10 Euler denoising steps at inference ([config.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/src/openpi/training/config.py), [optimizer.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/src/openpi/training/optimizer.py)). Two families of settings exist. **The large-batch recipes (`pi05_libero`, full DROID) hold LR constant at 5e-5 after warmup at batch 256**, with EMA 0.999 on LIBERO. **The small-dataset recipes (`pi05_aloha_pen_uncap`, `pi05_droid_finetune`) keep the default cosine schedule from 2.5e-5 down to 2.5e-6, at batch 32–64 for 20k steps** ([config.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/src/openpi/training/config.py)). The released `pi05_libero` checkpoint scored 96.85% averaged across LIBERO after 30k steps ([LIBERO README](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/examples/libero/README.md)). Action horizon is set per robot: 10 for LIBERO, 15–16 for DROID, and 50 for ALOHA.

LeRobot's port matches these defaults closely. It uses lr 2.5e-5, betas (0.9, 0.95), weight decay 0.01, clip 1.0, and cosine decay over 30k steps with 1k warmup. Chunk size and `n_action_steps` are both 50, and state and action both use QUANTILES normalization. When `--steps` is lower than the default, the scheduler scales down to match: 3k steps gets a 100-step warmup ([configuration_pi05.py](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/policies/pi05/configuration_pi05.py)). The documented LIBERO recipe trains everything with `freeze_vision_encoder=false`, `train_expert_only=false`, bf16, gradient checkpointing, batch 64, 30k steps, and **`n_action_steps=10`**. The docs say the expert-only variant uses "less memory, at some cost in success rate" ([LeRobot pi05 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/pi05)). PI's maintainer is blunter. Fine-tuning "likely will work a lot worse if you freeze the full VLM backbone and only finetune the action expert" ([openpi #649](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/issues/649)).

LoRA is the trap to avoid. In one community comparison, π0.5 LoRA at default rank matched full fine-tuning on training loss (about 0.0024) and on open-loop error, yet **scored 0/150 in closed loop against 60/150 for full fine-tuning**. The two runs also used different LR and EMA settings ([openpi #1060](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/issues/1060)). A controlled SO-101 study saw the same pattern: r32 LoRA stayed at 0–7% at every data size ([arXiv 2610.37170](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37170)). Knowledge insulation, which PI used to pretrain π0.5, does not exist at fine-tune time in openpi, and PI has "not seen conclusive evidence that it actually helps during finetuning" ([openpi #649](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/issues/649)).

The SO-101 rows come from [arXiv 2610.37170](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37170) and [5hadytru/so101_bench_pi05](https://huggingface.co/5hadytru/so101_bench_pi05). The SO-101 expert-only study ran each step at about **6.3 s on one A100-80GB in fp32 at batch 32, roughly 10.5 h for 6k steps**. Part of that slowness is a known LeRobot inefficiency: a pending fix (open PR) brings expert-only training from about 7 s/step to about 1.67 s/step at batch 16 ([PR #3706](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/pull/3706)). Full fine-tuning needs an 80 GB-class GPU. One user ran out of memory at batch 32 with `compile_model` on an A100-80GB and succeeded at batch 16 ([lerobot #2216](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/2216)). Avoid `compile_model` for training for now, because it can silently train on NaN loss ([lerobot #4178](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4178)).

## About 50 demos per task is enough, if the demos are consistent

Our ~212 episodes over 4 tasks is about 53 per instruction, averaging about 7 s each. That is a reasonable amount by SO-101 standards. In a controlled single-task study, expert-only π0.5 reached **40% at 12 demos, 70% at 25, and plateaued at 70–75% up to 200**. ACT overtook it at 100–200 demos (93%). π0.5's remaining failures were mostly lost grips ([arXiv 2610.37170](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37170)). The study's own caveat matters here: every π0.5 cell trained a fixed 6k steps, so the plateau partly reflects under-training. On multi-task SO-101 benchmarks, π0.5 led with **56% across 4 tasks at 100 demos each**, against 34% for ACT ([arXiv 2606.08881](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08881v2)). It also led with **47.6% across 12 tasks at 50 demos each** ([arXiv 2607.24481](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24481v1)). Per-task variance is large, though. In the first benchmark π0.5 scored 95% on pen transfer and 10% on colour sorting, and colour sorting is close to our three-bowl placement problem.

Consistency matters more than volume. The π0 paper says post-training data should show "a consistent and fluent strategy", and that recovery behaviour comes mainly from the diverse pretraining mix ([π0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164)). openpi filters idle timesteps by default for DROID because doing so "significantly improve[s] policy performance" ([DROID README_train](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/examples/droid/README_train.md)). A field study of π0 linked mid-task freezing to training frames of "holding" that are mostly idle, so the most common learned action at those states is to do nothing ([Penn PAL](https://penn-pal-lab.github.io/Pi0-Experiment-in-the-Wild/)). The SmolVLA team structures 50 episodes as 5 placements × 10 repeats and found 25 episodes "not enough" ([LeRobot SmolVLA docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/smolvla)). Two data rules follow. Failed grasps mixed into training make the action target ambiguous, so remove or relabel them ([OpenELAB guide](https://openelab.io/blogs/learn/how-to-fine-tune-smolvla-on-so-101-with-lerobot)). LeRobot image augmentation is off by default, while openpi applies colour jitter to every camera and crop/rotate to non-wrist cameras ([openpi model.py](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/src/openpi/models/model.py), [LeRobot dataset docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/lerobot-dataset-v3)). A LeRobot fine-tune therefore runs without augmentation unless someone enables it.

Language conditioning is our second risk after the gripper. Small π0.5 fine-tunes often treat the prompt as a task selector or ignore it altogether. One user's 250-episode fine-tune did the same task whatever the prompt said ([openpi #768](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/issues/768)). A 3-task × 30-demo run ignored language at 30k steps, but earlier checkpoints still responded to it ([openpi #864](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/issues/864)). Our three place instructions differ only in a colour word. If the scene looks the same for all three, only the language can disambiguate them, and an over-trained or expert-only policy can collapse them into one behaviour. Adding cameras can also weaken grounding. On ALOHA, OpenVLA-OFT latched onto visual correlations and its language following fell to chance until FiLM was added ([OpenVLA-OFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19645v2)). So the `inspect` camera, which is in-distribution only for the present task, deserves a deliberate check.

## Two policies that never close the gripper point to a shared cause

"Reaches the object, never closes" is the most-reported π0.5 fine-tune failure, and no maintainer has diagnosed its root cause. In one report, π0.5 trained on about 200 episodes reached the object, output tiny actions, pushed it, and never grasped, even though open-loop replay looked fine. The suggested checks were to log the raw gripper output before de-normalization and to confirm that the norm stats match training exactly ([openpi #912](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/issues/912)). In another, a 0/0.3 gripper encoding gave accurate joints but poor gripper predictions, and the fix suggested was to match PI's pretraining convention, 0 = open and 1 = closed ([openpi #850](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/issues/850), [norm_stats.md](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/docs/norm_stats.md)). On SO-101, the follower reports body joints in degrees and the gripper on a 0–100 range. Recording and deployment must agree on `use_degrees` and on calibration ([so_follower config](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/robots/so_follower/config_so_follower.py)). One pooled-corpus builder rejected datasets whose actions were in the leader-arm frame, because "normalization statistics absorb scale but not a shifted zero point" ([dongyoonkim/so101-pi05-base](https://huggingface.co/dongyoonkim/so101-pi05-base)). Quantile normalization maps q01–q99 to [-1, 1]. A dimension that rarely moves can get a tiny range and blow up after normalization ([openpi README](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/main/README.md)). Conversely, if closing is rare, a closed value can sit near the q99 edge, where it saturates.

ACT and π0.5 share data, calibration, robot driver and camera stack, and both fail at the same moment. That argues against a model-specific bug and for something shared. Our hypotheses, in order of likelihood, are inferences rather than established findings. **(1) Closure timing in the data.** If operators pause, hover or approach slowly before closing, the grasp state looks like "do nothing" (the Penn PAL mechanism), so the policy hovers. **(2) Gripper encoding and offsets.** The leader gripper's 0–100 range may not map to the follower's closure range, or calibration may differ between record time and run time. **(3) Approach precision.** Neither policy may get close enough for the closure frames to be in distribution. Being 6–7 cm short in sim is consistent with this, and the gripper never closing is then a consequence rather than the cause. **(4) Deployment-only factors.** These are a norm-stats mismatch, INT8 quantization flattening the sparse gripper dimension, and long open-loop chunks. On chunk length the evidence is concrete: π0 itself re-plans every 16 steps at 20 Hz or every 25 at 50 Hz, and found temporal ensembling harmful ([π0 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.24164)). SmolVLA on LIBERO fell from 82.8% to 51.8% when it executed all 50 steps instead of 10 ([SmolVLA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01844)). Changing a LeRobot SmolVLA checkpoint from `n_action_steps` 50 to 10 gained 20–23 pp ([lerobot #4614](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4614)). LeRobot's `--policy.pretrained_path` silently resets `n_action_steps` to 50 ([LeRobot pi05 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/pi05)). Hidden camera latency of several hundred ms also biased π0.5's approaches until it was fixed ([openpi #906](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/issues/906)).

The LeRobot port has its own known issues. Users report jitter compared with OpenPI on the same data, which contributors attribute to absolute vs delta actions and to padded action dimensions. Those dimensions are excluded from the loss but still integrated at inference, where they leak noise back into the real joints. Fixes are in open PRs ([lerobot #2559](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/2559), [#5335](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/5335)). Separately, episode-end padding trains the model toward a frozen final pose ([#5105](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/5105)). A missing middle camera shifts later cameras into the wrong slots ([#5118](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/5118)). Keep all three of our cameras present in a fixed order.

## Offline MAE and sim results are bug detectors, not scores

Validation loss and action error predict closed-loop success poorly. For Diffusion Policy and ACT, lower validation loss often came with *lower* success ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/m1b/validation-loss-robotics)). For flow policies specifically, validation loss is "not a good proxy" ([arXiv 2512.01809](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01809)). The LoRA case above had the same loss and open-loop error as full fine-tuning yet 0 successes against 60. Restricting MSE to task-critical intervals improved its correlation with success from −0.61 to −0.87 ([arXiv 2606.29898](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29898)). Our ~7° MAE averaged over all frames therefore says almost nothing. The useful offline numbers are gripper-dimension error and closure timing in the 1–2 s window around contact, and the gap between running the training stack and running the deployed OpenVINO stack on the same episodes. A large gap means a plumbing bug.

Simulation is only good for ranking. SO-101 Bench's Isaac Lab twin ranked policies the same as the real robot (ρ = 1.00), but π0.5 scored 49% in sim against 18% on the real arm ([arXiv 2610.36420](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36420v1)). Our sim scene is approximate and was never part of training, so 0/3 there is weak evidence either way. The standard real-robot protocol is 10–24 trials per condition from a fixed grid of randomized placements, scored with partial credit. SmolVLA, for example, gives 0.5 for the grasp and 0.5 for the place ([OpenVLA-OFT](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19645v2), [SmolVLA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01844)). Use paired comparisons from identical start states when testing a single change ([lerobot #4614](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4614)). Pick checkpoints by rollouts. In SO-101 Bench the best checkpoint was step 12k of 18k ([5hadytru/so101_bench_pi05](https://huggingface.co/5hadytru/so101_bench_pi05)). PI's maintainer warned that 100k steps on a small dataset "very likely" overfits, when PI's own examples use 20–30k on larger data ([openpi #427](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/issues/427)).

## Corrections and RTC beat RL at hackathon scale

**RTC is the cheapest real improvement.** It runs at inference time only and needs no retraining. It inpaints the next chunk while freezing the actions already committed, and it was tested with π0.5 on real tasks under more than 300 ms of latency ([arXiv 2506.07339](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07339v2)). LeRobot exposes it with `--inference.type=rtc` and a guidance weight of about 10 for 10-step flow ([LeRobot RTC docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/en/rtc)). Without RTC, π0.5 on SO-101 paused about 0.17 s between chunks, against about 0.01 s for ACT ([arXiv 2610.37170](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37170)). Intel publishes a π0.5 + RTC OpenVINO pipeline whose exported model takes `prev_chunk_left_over` and `prefix_weights` as inputs ([Intel Open Edge docs](https://docs.openedgeplatform.intel.com/2026.1/edge-ai-suites/robotics-ai-suite/embodied/sample_pipelines/pi05_with_rtc.html)). That pipeline lands directly on our Arc GPU track. LeRobot can also train for RTC via `rtc_training_max_delay` ([LeRobot pi05 docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/pi05)). Our 135–178 ms per chunk is 4–6 frames at 30 Hz, so a training delay of about 6 fits.

**Human corrections are the most practical step beyond SFT.** In HG-DAgger style, we run the fine-tuned policy, take over with the leader arm when it fails, record the correction, and fine-tune again on demos plus corrections. That reuses our existing record and train tools. RLinf-USER supports this loop for π0 on a real Franka ([RLinf-USER](https://rlinf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/rst_source/resources/publications/rlinf_user.html)). **RECAP** (π*0.6) builds on the same data. It trains a value function on success and failure labels, turns per-action advantages into an "improvement indicator", and conditions the policy on "good" at inference. PI reports it more than doubles throughput and roughly halves failures on its hardest tasks ([arXiv 2511.14759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14759)). RLinf's offline π0.5 implementation raised a few-shot LIBERO task from 48.8% to 66.5% in one iteration ([RLinf RECAP docs](https://rlinf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/rst_source/examples/embodied/recap.html)). It targets OpenPI configs, though, and porting it to SO-101 means a new data config. Treat it as a stretch.

**Online RL is out of scope.** πRL's gains, such as LIBERO π0.5 rising from 77.1% to 97.9%, depend on massively parallel simulators and multi-GPU nodes ([RLinf π0 docs](https://rlinf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/rst_source/examples/embodied/pi0.html)). DSRL runs SAC in noise space with the VLA frozen ([arXiv 2506.15799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15799)). It is the only real-robot RL option that is plausible for us, but no one has shown it on SO-101. LeRobot's HIL-SERL does support SO-101, but it trains a small SAC actor rather than π0.5 ([LeRobot HIL-SERL docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/hilserl)). A cheap variant of value-guided selection is to sample N chunks and keep the best one by a grasp/progress classifier. V-GPS reported +82% in the real world with this approach ([arXiv 2410.13816](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13816)). It costs N× inference, which our latency budget does not allow on the Arc GPU.

## Recommended plan for the next fine-tune and deployment

The plan below is our synthesis. Compute figures are estimates scaled from the cited runs. The relevant data points are about 6.3 s/step for expert-only fp32 at batch 32 on an A100 ([arXiv 2610.37170](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37170)), about 3–4 h for 5k steps of an openpi fine-tune on an A100 ([abdul004/pi05_so101_checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/abdul004/pi05_so101_checkpoint)), and 36 h on 4×A100 for 18k steps at batch 256 ([5hadytru/so101_bench_pi05](https://huggingface.co/5hadytru/so101_bench_pi05)). Our own s/step on Modal is the number to measure in the first 100 steps.

Pick the best checkpoint, not the last. | About 1 h per checkpoint; 2–4 h total |

Use simulation only as a smoke test for crashes and gross errors. The decisive evaluation is P3 on the real arm. If P0 finds a data or encoding bug, the ACT baseline can be retrained on the fixed data within a couple of hours as a cross-check.

## Conclusion

Our current fine-tune deviates from established practice on three counts: it trains about 10× too few steps, freezes the backbone, and measures success by offline MAE and an untrained sim scene. Even so, none of the three explains why ACT and π0.5 both fail at grasp closure. That shared symptom changes the priority. The cheapest high-value action is a few hours of forensics on the gripper channel and the deployed inference path, not a bigger GPU run. If the data shows hesitant closures or a mismatched gripper range, every downstream model inherits the bug, and only fixing the data or the mapping helps.

The second lesson is that π0.5's advantage over ACT is strongest at low data, and that this advantage depends on running it as practitioners do. That means a full or at least unfrozen fine-tune, short executed chunks or RTC, and a checkpoint chosen by robot trials. Language binding across three colour-only instructions is the next risk to test explicitly once the policy grasps. Post-training beyond SFT is worth one round of corrections, and nothing more ambitious, before judging.
