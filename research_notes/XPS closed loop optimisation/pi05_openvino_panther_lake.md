# Lowest-latency π0.5 on Core Ultra X9 388H (Panther Lake, Arc B390 / 12 Xe3, NPU 5) with OpenVINO 2026.x, as of Nov 2026

Scope: these notes build on `reports/XPU kernels and Chinese VLAs.md` and do not repeat it. They add source-level reading of three repos: Physical AI Studio `library/` at HEAD 2026-11-12, the `physicalai` 0.4.0 wheel from PyPI, and robotics-ai-suite `pi05-rtc-ov` at HEAD 2026-11-13 with its patches applied to the pinned LeRobot. They also add OpenVINO GPU-plugin source at tags 2026.1.0–2026.4.0 and on master, figure values read from Intel's paper, and the first third-party Panther Lake π0.5 numbers (Nota AI).

Our reference point is PyTorch-XPU bf16 `lerobot/pi05_base` on the XPS: 467 ms at 10 steps, 382 ms at 5 and 313 ms at 1. That is about 296 ms of prefix (vision + VLM) plus about 17 ms per denoising step.

Four corrections to the earlier report come first:
- **FuseGatedMLP is not in shipped OpenVINO.** In 2026.2–2026.4 it is a debug-only option, disabled by default, so release builds cannot run it. Master removed it on 2026-10-28.
- **The NPU split is slower than iGPU-only in synchronous mode** on this chip family, per third-party measurement.
- **OpenVINO 2026.3 has a π0.5 performance regression.** Use 2026.4.x.
- **In-graph noise is broken on the GPU plugin.** Each call returns the same random values, so the start noise must be a graph input.

## 1. Physical AI Studio export of `physicalai.policies.Pi05` and the `physicalai` runtime

### Takeaway
Studio exports π0.5 to OpenVINO as **one monolithic, static-shape IR**. The pipeline is torch → ONNX → OV, with FP16 weights, plus a separate `tokenizer.xml`. The runner is `SinglePass`. The camera count, `tokenizer_max_length` (default **200**) and `num_inference_steps` (unrolled) are all baked in. RTC is exported **in-graph** as a guidance correction without a VJP, with scalar control inputs. INT8 is available only as an optional post-export hook (`INT8_SYM`). Nothing in it emits a split IR, uses INT4 or targets the NPU. The runtime compiles with whatever kwargs you pass. It sets no LATENCY hint or CACHE_DIR by default, and its `RTCExecution` runs inference on a background thread with `inference_delay = ceil(max_latency × fps)`.

### Cited Findings
- `Pi05.get_supported_export_backends()` returns only `[TORCH, OPENVINO]`. The OpenVINO params are `OpenVINOExportParameters(compress_to_fp16=True, via_onnx=True, export_tokenizer=True)`, with a preprocessor chain of `normalize` → `pi05` (resize to `image_resolution`, `empty_cameras`) → optional `noise` → `ov_tokenizer` (`tokenizer.xml`), and a postprocessor of `denormalize` (+ `action_chunk_trimmer` if `n_action_steps < chunk_size`) — [physical-ai-studio `library/src/physicalai/policies/pi05/policy.py`](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/src/physicalai/policies/pi05/policy.py)
- `to_openvino()` converts on CPU. It builds `input_shapes` from the static sample tensors, calls `openvino.save_model(..., compress_to_fp16=...)`, exports the tokenizer with `openvino_tokenizers.convert_tokenizer(..., max_length=max_token_len, use_max_padding=True)`, and writes a manifest with `runner=SinglePass`. That makes it a single graph, not a VE/LM/AE split — [`library/src/physicalai/export/mixin_policy.py`](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/src/physicalai/export/mixin_policy.py)
- The quantisation hook is `physicalai.export.hooks.compress_weights_openvino_int8_sym(model_path)`, which applies `nncf.compress_weights(ov_model, mode=INT8_SYM)` and is passed as `policy.export(..., post_export_hooks=[...])`. Its only callers are tests, and there is no INT4 hook — [`library/src/physicalai/export/hooks.py`](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/src/physicalai/export/hooks.py)
- Inputs schema: `state`, one image input per camera (`images.<name>`), `task` (a string, tokenised in OV), and `noise` `(chunk_size, max_action_dim)` when `use_random_input_noise=True` (the default). A code comment explains the noise input: *"The start noise is a graph input: OpenVINO's GPU plugin repeats in-graph random values."* When RTC is enabled the schema adds `prev_chunk_left_over` `(chunk, action_dim)`, `inference_delay` (int64 scalar), `max_guidance_weight` (f32 scalar) and `execution_horizon` (int64 scalar) — [`policy.py`](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/src/physicalai/policies/pi05/policy.py)
- Config defaults: `tokenizer_max_length=200`, `image_resolution=(224,224)`, `num_inference_steps=10`, `chunk_size=50`, `empty_cameras=0`, `dtype="bfloat16"` — [`pi05/config.py`](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/src/physicalai/policies/pi05/config.py)
- At inference (`not self.training`), `embed_prefix` batches all cameras into **one SigLIP call** (`images.reshape(num_cameras*bsize, ...)`). `sample_actions` unrolls `for step in range(num_steps)` using `snapflow_num_inference_steps()`, so a SnapFlow checkpoint exports a single step. It forces `_attn_implementation="eager"` on the vision tower, the LM and the expert. RTC is applied per step through `_compute_prefix_weights` and `_rtc_correct`, with no autograd — [`pi05/model.py`](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/src/physicalai/policies/pi05/model.py)
- The prompt is `"Task: {task}, State: {discretised state};\nAction: "`. The state is digitised into 256 bins over its **raw** dimensions, not padded to `max_state_dim`, so the SO-101 contributes 6 numbers — [`pi05/preprocessor.py`](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/src/physicalai/policies/pi05/preprocessor.py); [physicalai 0.4.0 `inference/preprocessors/pi05.py` (PyPI wheel)](https://pypi.org/project/physicalai/)
- Runtime `OpenVINOAdapter.load()` calls `core.compile_model(model, device, config=_compile_config(device, self.config))`, where `self.config` holds the extra kwargs passed to `InferenceModel(export_dir, device=..., **adapter_kwargs)`. The only default it injects is f32 on ARM CPUs. `predict()` calls `self.compiled_model(inputs)` with numpy dicts, which means a host copy each call and no remote tensors — [physicalai 0.4.0 `inference/adapters/openvino.py`](https://pypi.org/project/physicalai/)
- `RTCExecution(chunk_size=None, execution_horizon=15, fps=30.0, max_guidance_weight=5.0, queue_threshold=None, latency_tracker=None, warmup_inferences=2)`. Its docstring says the defaults were "validated on π0.5 at 30 fps". It injects `noise`, `prev_chunk_left_over`, `inference_delay`, `max_guidance_weight` and `execution_horizon`. When `queue_threshold=None` the threshold is `execution_horizon + latency_delay_actions`. `RTCLatencyTracker.compute_delay(fps) = ceil(max_latency_s × fps)`, taken over a sliding window. `AsyncExecution(request_threshold=0.5)` runs a background thread — [physicalai 0.4.0 `runtime/execution/rtc.py`, `async_execution.py`, `inference/callbacks/rtc_latency.py`](https://pypi.org/project/physicalai/)
- Recent runtime fixes. PR #442 (merged 2026-11-06): *"pi05 exports sampled the flow-matching start noise inside the graph. OpenVINO's GPU plugin returns the same random values on every call… on an Intel GPU every action chunk started from the same noise"*. On an iGPU bucket-sort task this gave 5/20–18/20 success with in-graph noise against 16–18/20 after the fix (torch: 18/20). PR #410 (2026-10-27) fixed "RTC periodic replay" of stale observations — [physicalai PR #442](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/physicalai/pull/442); [PR #410](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/physicalai/pull/410)
- OpenVINO version pins. PR #229 (2026-08-10) set OV ≥ 2026.3 because "all previous versions had critical bugs related to" SmolVLA or pi05. PR #283 (2026-09-18) moved to 2026.4 "because of perf regression in pi05, which is fixed in .4. .3 is excluded". The OpenVINO 2026.4 release notes say "Updated OpenVINO dependencies fix… a slowdown in pi05 inference" — [physicalai PR #283](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/physicalai/pull/283); [PR #229](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/physicalai/pull/229); [OpenVINO release notes](https://docs.openvino.ai/releasenotes)

### Inferences
- Without changes, a Studio export of our policy has 3×256 image tokens plus **200** padded language tokens, so **968 prefix tokens**. The real prompt for SO-101 (task plus 6 state numbers) is probably under 48 tokens. Lowering `tokenizer_max_length` to about 48–64 before export would cut the prefix by roughly 14–16%. Intel's paper used 64. Padding tokens are masked, so the output should match as long as nothing is truncated, but trajectory MSE against PyTorch should confirm that.
- Pass the latency config through `InferenceModel(..., device="GPU", PERFORMANCE_HINT="LATENCY", INFERENCE_PRECISION_HINT="f16", CACHE_DIR=...)`, because the adapter forwards kwargs into `compile_model`. That is our reading of the code and has not been tested.
- Any Studio export made before the noise-input change, or with `use_random_input_noise=False`, should be re-exported. Otherwise every chunk on the GPU starts from the same noise.

### Gaps
- No Intel-published latency for a Studio-exported π0.5 on any Intel GPU was found. Studio's own SnapFlow latency numbers are H100 Torch numbers.
- I did not verify whether `physicalai export` exposes INT8 on the CLI. In code it is only a Python hook.

## 2. robotics-ai-suite `pi05-rtc-ov` (convert_ov.py / convert_ov_rtc.py)

### Takeaway
The public pipeline is a set of git patches on LeRobot **v0.4.3** (submodule commit `0b067df`), pinned to `openvino==2026.0`. Both converters emit **one monolithic IR** (`model.xml`) per camera count and precision. `convert_ov.py` runs on CPU, casts bf16 to fp32, traces with eager attention and saves FP16 by default, with an optional NNCF `INT8_ASYM`. The public repo has **no GPU/NPU split, no KV-cache handoff code, no INT4, and no compile hints**. The split described in Intel's paper is not in this repo.

### Cited Findings
- Layout: `README.md`, `patches/0001…0009*.patch` and a `lerobot` submodule. The pinned commit is `0b067df57d21d3a02d6c511f1609172fa39ac29b`, and patch 0001 shows LeRobot `version = "0.4.3"`, `requires-python >=3.10,<3.11`, `openvino==2026.0` and a transformers fork `fix/lerobot_openpi`. The patches date from 2026-01-12 to 2026-05-28 — [robotics-ai-suite pi05-rtc-ov](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/robotics-ai-suite/tree/main/src/pipelines/pi05-rtc-ov)
- `convert_ov_rtc.py` loads `PI05Policy` on CPU and strips the config keys `use_peft`, `freeze_vision_encoder` and `train_expert_only`. It sets `RTCConfig(enabled=True, execution_horizon=10, max_guidance_weight=5.0, prefix_attention_schedule=EXP)` and wraps `sample_actions`. In the wrapper, `embed_prefix` produces the prefix, `language_model.forward(..., use_cache=True)` with `_attn_implementation="eager"` produces `past_key_values`, and a Python loop over `num_inference_steps` (unrolled at trace time) calls `denoise_step` plus the RTC guidance. The guidance is `v_t − min(c·inv_r2, β)·(prev − (x_t − t·v_t))·prefix_weights`, with no VJP. The script then casts all bf16 params and buffers to fp32 and runs `ov.convert_model(wrapper, example_input=…, input=[static shapes])`. Inputs are `images [N_cam,1,3,224,224]`, `img_masks`, `lang_tokens [1, tokenizer_max_length]`, `lang_masks`, `noise [1,50,32]`, `prev_chunk_left_over [1,50,32]` and `prefix_weights [1,50,1]`, with output `actions_out`. It saves to `<out>_{N}c_FP16/model.xml`, and with `--compress_int8` also to `<out>_{N}c_INT8` via `nncf.compress_weights(mode=INT8_ASYM)`. Validation compares against PyTorch on **CPU** with `execution_mode=ACCURACY` — patched `examples/pi05_with_openvino/scripts/convert_ov_rtc.py` (from [patch 0002/0004](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/robotics-ai-suite/tree/main/src/pipelines/pi05-rtc-ov/patches))
- `convert_ov.py` is the same without the RTC inputs (`images, img_masks, lang_tokens, lang_masks, noise`) — same source
- KV cache: inside the single IR the prefix KV is an intermediate tensor. Nothing is passed between separate models — same source
- Runtime integration in the patched `modeling_pi05.py`: when `ov_config` is set it calls `core.compile_model(read_model(model_dir/model.xml), device_name=ov_config.device)` with **no properties**, creates one infer request and calls `infer_request.infer(inputs)` synchronously. Zero tensors disable RTC. `OVConfig.device` defaults to `"CPU"` — patched `src/lerobot/policies/pi05/modeling_pi05.py`, `configuration_pi05.py` ([patch 0003](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/robotics-ai-suite/tree/main/src/pipelines/pi05-rtc-ov/patches))
- Patch 0008 (2026-05-28), "Set pos-embedding dtype to float32 for performance", changes the sinusoidal time embedding from float64 to float32 — [patch 0008](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/robotics-ai-suite/tree/main/src/pipelines/pi05-rtc-ov/patches)
- The README's `benchmark_pi05_ov_rtc.py --device GPU --chunk_size 75 -n 10` times preprocessing, inference and postprocessing with `time.time()`. The README publishes **no latency numbers**, and `--camera_num` defaults to 4 — [README](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/robotics-ai-suite/tree/main/src/pipelines/pi05-rtc-ov)
- The older OEP CPU recipe (`edge-ai-suites` `vla-pi0.5-openvino`, OV 2025.4, NNCF 2.19) goes through ONNX. It patches `sample_noise` to zeros and uses `INT8_ASYM, all_layers=True`, benchmarking with `lang_tokens[1,200]` — [OEP: OpenVINO Optimization of Robotics VLA Model Pi0.5](https://docs.openedgeplatform.intel.com/2026.1/edge-ai-suites/robotics-ai-suite/embodied/openvino_optimization.html)

### Inferences
- **LeRobot 0.7 / Studio checkpoints:** the converter imports `lerobot.policies.pi05.modeling_pi05.PI05Policy` from the patched 0.4.3 tree. A LeRobot 0.4.x-format `config.json` + safetensors should load (the key-stripping exists for that). A Studio Lightning `.ckpt`, or LeRobot 0.7 checkpoints with renamed keys or processors, would need conversion first. Studio's own `physicalai export` is the native path for Studio checkpoints. This is untested.
- The public RTC converter uses a NumPy-free guidance formula with `prefix_weights` computed on the host, while Studio passes `inference_delay`/`execution_horizon` scalars and computes the weights in-graph. The two exports are not interchangeable at the runtime level.
- `compress_int8` here is `INT8_ASYM`. The Nota NPU work found that the NPU compiler stores asymmetric INT8 weights at FP16 size (section 4), so use `INT8_SYM` for anything that targets the NPU.

### Gaps
- The VE/LE/AE split IRs, the USM-host KV wiring and the per-stage numbers in Intel's paper are not in any public Intel repo I found. The paper points to "Configuration Guide for the Optimized OpenVINO Model" and "Heterogeneous Computing with Zero-Copy Between NPU and GPU", but no URLs are embedded in the PDF and I could not locate either document.

## 3. OpenVINO 2026.x GPU-plugin features that matter on Xe3

### Takeaway
On Xe3 (`supports_immad`): oneDNN is on, the queue is in-order, the inference precision is **f16** (bf16 is not an accepted GPU `inference_precision` value), and SDPA uses micro-kernels for head_dim ≤ 256, which covers Gemma's 256. Dynamic per-token INT8 activation quantisation applies only to FCs with **compressed weights** and **more than 64 rows**. That means the 968-token prefix gets W8A8 XMX, and the 50-token expert stays weight-dequant FP16. KV-cache compression applies only to stateful or paged LLM graphs, not a stateless π0.5 IR. **The FuseGatedMLP pass cited in Intel's paper cannot be enabled in release wheels.**

### Cited Findings
- FuseGatedMLP history: PR #34427 "[GPU] Gated mlp" (merged 2026-03-18) added a oneDNN `gated_mlp` primitive and the `FuseGatedMLP` pass, with *"Default fusion is disable (disable_gated_mlp_fusion: TRUE)"*. At tags 2026.2.0–2026.4.0 the switch is `OV_CONFIG_DEBUG_OPTION(ov::intel_gpu, disable_gated_mlp_fusion, true, …)`, read with `GPU_DEBUG_VALUE_OR(config.get_disable_gated_mlp_fusion(), true)`, and registered only `if (device_info.supports_immad && config.get_use_onednn() && !disable_gated_mlp_fusion)`. In non-debug builds `GPU_DEBUG_VALUE_OR(debug, release)` evaluates to the release value `true`, so the pass never runs. PR #39202 (merged 2026-10-28) **removed** the GatedMLP op, the primitive and FuseGatedMLP: *"We decided to use binary-mul-inplace instead"* — [PR #34427](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/pull/34427); [PR #39202](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/pull/39202); [options.inl @2026.4.0](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/2026.4.0/src/plugins/intel_gpu/include/intel_gpu/runtime/options.inl); [transformations_pipeline.cpp @2026.4.0](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/2026.4.0/src/plugins/intel_gpu/src/plugin/transformations_pipeline.cpp)
- The horizontal gate/up FC + SwiGLU merge (`fuse_mlp_swiglu`) is enabled only when `!config.get_use_onednn() && !device_info.supports_immad && EUs >= 128`, so **not on XMX iGPUs** like Xe3. On Xe3, `GLUFusion` (Swish×Mul → SwiGLU) and FC horizontal fusion still run — [transformations_pipeline.cpp (master)](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/master/src/plugins/intel_gpu/src/plugin/transformations_pipeline.cpp)
- SDPA: `enable_sdpa_optimization` defaults to true. On XMX, SDPA is kept fused if `query_microkernels_supported(...) && head_size <= 256`, unless a 3-D SDPA's Q·K buffer is small enough to decompose. The data type must be f16 or bf16, and a boolean attention mask on non-causal SDPA has extra conditions. `ExpandBroadcastReshapeSDPAFusion`, `SDPAScaleFusion` and `SDPATransposeFusion` (which absorbs the output transpose) are registered — [transformations_pipeline.cpp (master)](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/master/src/plugins/intel_gpu/src/plugin/transformations_pipeline.cpp); [options.inl @2026.4.0](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/2026.4.0/src/plugins/intel_gpu/include/intel_gpu/runtime/options.inl)
- Both Studio and pi05-rtc-ov trace with `_attn_implementation="eager"`, so SDPA in the IR comes only from OV's pattern fusion of MatMul–Softmax–MatMul, not from a native `aten::scaled_dot_product_attention` — Studio [`model.py`](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/src/physicalai/policies/pi05/model.py); pi05-rtc-ov `convert_ov_rtc.py`
- Nota AI's Panther Lake prefix IR was compiled with `GPU_ENABLE_SDPA_OPTIMIZATION=NO` plus `PERFORMANCE_HINT=LATENCY`. The card gives no reason — [HF geonmin-kim/nota-pi05-libero-vtp-drift-openvino](https://huggingface.co/geonmin-kim/nota-pi05-libero-vtp-drift-openvino)
- Precision: the 2026.4 option is `inference_precision` default `f16`, with "Supported values: { f16, f32, dynamic }". The device can report a `BF16` capability, but bf16 is not a selectable GPU inference precision. `EXECUTION_MODE_HINT=ACCURACY` sets `dynamic` (IR precision) — [options.inl @2026.4.0](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/2026.4.0/src/plugins/intel_gpu/include/intel_gpu/runtime/options.inl); [execution_config.cpp @2026.4.0](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/2026.4.0/src/plugins/intel_gpu/src/runtime/execution_config.cpp); [plugin.cpp](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/master/src/plugins/intel_gpu/src/plugin/plugin.cpp)
- Dynamic quantisation: on `supports_immad` devices, if the user leaves it unset, `dynamic_quantization_group_size = UINT64_MAX` (per-token). Non-XMX devices get 32. The internal `dynamic_quantization_threshold = 64` means "apply only when batch size is larger than this value in OneDNN". The pass skips FCs with f32 inputs. On arch ≥ Xe2, INT8 per-token uses gs=128 — [execution_config.cpp @2026.4.0](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/2026.4.0/src/plugins/intel_gpu/src/runtime/execution_config.cpp); [transformations_pipeline.cpp](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/master/src/plugins/intel_gpu/src/plugin/transformations_pipeline.cpp)
- KV-cache precision logic (u4 for paged-attention + 4-bit weights, i8 otherwise, by-channel key quantisation) is tied to stateful KV-cache/PagedAttention models. 2026.2 enabled INT4 KV-cache compression for GPUs, aimed at prompts longer than 32K — [execution_config.cpp @2026.4.0](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/2026.4.0/src/plugins/intel_gpu/src/runtime/execution_config.cpp); [OpenVINO 2026.2 release](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/releases/tag/2026.2.0)
- Release timeline: 2026.1 (7 Apr), 2026.2 (28 May; NNCF "Added INT4/INT8 weight compression support for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) with Pi0.5 model"; first OpenVINO Physical AI release), 2026.3 (4 Aug), 2026.4 (16 Sep; "Xe3 integrated graphics optimizations… for Gemma 4 models processing long-context inputs", which is GenAI only; NPU ITT/VTune profiling; physicalai pi05 slowdown fix) — [OpenVINO release notes](https://docs.openvino.ai/releasenotes); [GitHub releases](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/releases)
- The capability list exposes `HW_MATMUL` (immad) and `USM_MEMORY`. `ov::intel_gpu::ocl::USMTensor` exists in the remote-tensor API — [plugin.cpp](https://github.com/openvinotoolkit/openvino/blob/master/src/plugins/intel_gpu/src/plugin/plugin.cpp); [OpenVINO docs](https://docs.openvino.ai/releasenotes)

### Inferences
- Recommended compile config for the monolithic or prefix IR on the B390 (untested): `{"PERFORMANCE_HINT":"LATENCY","INFERENCE_PRECISION_HINT":"f16","CACHE_DIR":"<dir>"}`, on OpenVINO **2026.4.x** (not 2026.3). Leave dynamic quantisation at its default; it activates only on INT8/INT4-weight FCs in the prefix. A/B test `GPU_ENABLE_SDPA_OPTIMIZATION=NO` against the default, because Nota chose `NO` on this exact iGPU.
- Our PyTorch baseline runs bf16. OV GPU will run f16. Gemma activations can overflow in f16, and the plugin already keeps RMS inputs, sin generation and gated residuals in fp32 (`disable_fp16_comp_*`, `increase_rms_input_precision`). Validate actions against bf16 PyTorch (Studio's noise-seeded export makes this deterministic).
- The "GatedMLP fusion" step in Intel's 555 → 172 ms progression was probably run on a debug or custom build. Do not expect it from `pip install openvino`. The replacement path (binary-mul-inplace on master) is not in a release yet.
- Remote tensors and USM zero-copy matter only for a split pipeline (KV handoff). For the monolithic IR the inputs are about 3×150 KB of images plus a few KB, so host copies are negligible next to 100+ ms of compute.

### Gaps
- I found no Xe3-specific GPU-plugin release-note item for non-GenAI graphs, and no published measurement of FP16 vs f32 accuracy for π0.5 on the GPU.
- Whether the 2026.3 pi05 regression was in the GPU plugin, the ONNX frontend or the tokenizers is not stated.

## 4. GPU + NPU split (VE+LLM on iGPU, AE on NPU) on Panther Lake

### Takeaway
It works, and third parties have built it (Nota AI, OV 2026.4, X7 358H). But **in synchronous mode it is slower than iGPU-only** (base 10-step: 330 ms vs 250 ms median; 1-step: 129 vs 105 ms). The FP16 action expert takes 144 ms per 10 steps on the NPU, against about 9 ms per step on the iGPU, and accuracy is unchanged. The split can only pay off with async/RTC overlap, which nobody has measured. With SnapFlow at 1 step the expert is a small slice of latency, so the split has almost nothing to hide.

### Cited Findings
- Intel's paper says the split turns the refill bottleneck from T_VE+T_LE+T_AE into max(T_VE+T_LE, T_AE) under RTC. It also says "since the iGPU and NPU share a single memory controller, each device experiences reduced throughput under concurrent workloads… max() represents an upper bound". On LIBERO-10 "the GPU+NPU serial split matches the monolithic iGPU baseline in success rate while freeing the iGPU". Its stated benefits are power and freeing the iGPU — "running AE on the NPU saves power" — and it publishes no millisecond figures for the split — [Intel π0.5 paper §5](https://docs.openedgeplatform.intel.com/shared_media/publication-optimize-pi0.5-paper.pdf)
- Nota AI split on X7 358H / Arc B390 / NPU 5 (Windows 11, OV 2026.4.0, `PERFORMANCE_HINT=LATENCY`). The IRs are `prefix.xml` (VE + LM → KV cache, iGPU) and `denoise.xml` (one AE step, NPU) plus `meta.npz`, all FP16. Median of 10 sync runs: base 10-step **iGPU 250 ms vs iGPU+NPU 330 ms**; Drift (1-step) + VTP128 **105 ms vs 129 ms**. In-episode means including pre/post: base 258 vs 402 ms, Drift 175 vs 226 ms, Drift+VTP128 117 vs 154 ms. LIBERO-10: base 95.5% (iGPU) vs 94.0% (split). The NPU draws 22.5 W during the AE against 27.9 W on the iGPU, "but it is slower". About 70% of energy goes to the prefix. The card describes the split as intended for async RTC but publishes no async results — [HF geonmin-kim/nota-pi05-libero-vtp-drift-openvino](https://huggingface.co/geonmin-kim/nota-pi05-libero-vtp-drift-openvino)
- Nota AE-on-NPU optimisation (OV 2026.4.1, NNCF 3.4.0, NPU driver 32.0.100.5540, 64 GB LPDDR5-8533). The prefix KV is `key_00..17/value_00..17` of shape `[1,1,928,256]`, set once per chunk. AE 10-step loop latency: FP16 **144.2 ms** → graph-opt 125.2 → mixed quant (MLP INT4-sym per-channel, other INT8-sym) 104.8 → both **77.8 ms** (blob 867 → 210 MB). The tricks are: (1) rewrite Gemma's MQA `repeat_kv` as one 4-D Broadcast, which moves attention from SHAVE to DPU; (2) precompute the 37 adaRMS timestep Linears (27% of AE weights) into a table that is valid only for a fixed schedule; (3) use **symmetric** quantisation, because "with asymmetric INT8, the NPU compiler stores weights at FP16 size". `NPU_TURBO=YES` avoids a 13–22 ms AE slowdown in sequential heterogeneous pipelines, because the NPU downclocks while the iGPU runs. AE weights are larger than the about 4.5 MB of CMX and stream from DRAM every step. LIBERO (800 episodes): 96.4% reference against 96.5–97.0% for the NPU variants, not significant — [HF wjwjwjwj/nota-pi05-libero-ae-npu-openvino](https://huggingface.co/wjwjwjwj/nota-pi05-libero-ae-npu-openvino)
- OpenVINO 2026.4 adds bounded dynamic shapes on NPU, validated for vision only, and ITT/VTune profiling on the NPU. 2026.2 integrated NPU compiler 8.1 — [OpenVINO release notes](https://docs.openvino.ai/releasenotes)

### Inferences
- With 50-action chunks at 30 Hz and Studio's `execution_horizon=15` (a 500 ms window), an iGPU-only latency of 100–250 ms already fits inside a window, so per Intel's own §5 reasoning the split brings no latency benefit. It is worth it only if we shrink the horizon below about 8 actions (about 267 ms), keep 10 steps, or need the iGPU for something else, such as the inspect-camera anomaly model.
- If we split anyway, use Studio/Nota-style separate `prefix` and `denoise` IRs, `INT8_SYM`/`INT4_SYM` on the AE, `NPU_TURBO=YES` and `PERFORMANCE_HINT=LATENCY`, and run the prefix for chunk n+1 while the NPU denoises chunk n.

### Gaps
- No published async/RTC measurement of the split, no published contention measurement, and no public USM-host KV code. Nota's numbers are on X7 358H under Windows. Our X9 388H has the same 12-Xe3 B390 class GPU, but clocks and power may differ (unverified).

## 5. Model-level levers on this chip

### Takeaway
In order of payoff on Panther Lake: (1) **1-step distillation** (SnapFlow in Studio; "Drift" in Nota's run) removes about 9 of 10 expert steps at no measurable LIBERO loss. (2) **Fewer visual tokens or cameras**: VTP to 128 tokens per camera cut iGPU latency from about 175 to 105–117 ms but cost about 2 points on LIBERO-10, and dropping a camera removes 256 tokens. (3) **Shorter `tokenizer_max_length`** is free (an inference). (4) **INT8 weights** on the whole model, and INT4 only on the backbone MLPs. INT4 on the full stack is risky.

### Cited Findings
- The Studio SO-101 SnapFlow recipe (`configs/physicalai/pi05/so101/snapflow.yaml`) has two phases in one run. Phase 1 (epochs 0–4) is a full flow-matching fine-tune from `lerobot/pi05_base` (bf16, grad checkpointing, `compile_model: true`, batch 16, lr 2.5e-5). Phase 2 (epochs 5–7) is SnapFlow via `SnapFlowPhaseCallback(start_epoch=5, alpha=0.5, lambda_=0.1, num_inference_steps=1)`, which freezes the VLM and trains about 10% of params (311.9M of 3138.4M). The exported artifact "carries the 1-NFE sampling path" (`physicalai export --ckpt_path … --backend openvino`) — [snapflow.yaml](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/configs/physicalai/pi05/so101/snapflow.yaml); [snapflow_distillation.md](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/docs/how-to/training/snapflow_distillation.md)
- Studio's LIBERO result: a 20k-step SnapFlow student scored **97.55%** against the 10-step teacher's 97.05% (2,000 episodes, one seed), at 58.9 against 175.4 ms per chunk on an H100 (Torch export, 2.98×). The doc notes the 0.5 pp difference "does not establish a statistically significant improvement" — [snapflow-libero.md](https://github.com/open-edge-platform/physical-ai-studio/blob/main/library/docs/explanation/benchmark/snapflow-libero.md)
- On Panther Lake OV iGPU (Nota): Drift 1-step reached 94.0% on LIBERO-10 against Base 95.5%. Drift + VTP K=128 (a learned sampler after the SigLIP projector, keeping 128 of 256 tokens per camera; prefix 968 → 584 tokens) reached 92.0%. Nota notes that compressed models score 1.5–3.5 points below Base on LIBERO-10. With in-graph ONNX-frontend noise (a Box-Muller bug reusing one uniform, mean 0.057, std 1.06) VTP fell to 83.5% and Drift to 90.5%, before Nota fixed it by feeding noise as an input — [HF nota-pi05-libero-vtp-drift-openvino](https://huggingface.co/geonmin-kim/nota-pi05-libero-vtp-drift-openvino)
- Intel's roofline projection for the PTL iGPU (Fig. 4, values read from the figure) gives VE/LE/AE ms. **2 cameras**: W16A16 16/47/62 (125 total), W8A16 12/47/37 (96), W8A8 9/24/37 (70). **3 cameras**: W16A16 24/68/66 (158), W8A16 18/68/40 (126), W8A8 13/35/40 (88). The configuration is 64 language tokens and 10 steps. Going from 3 to 2 cameras is about −20 to −25% in projection — [Intel π0.5 paper Fig. 4](https://docs.openedgeplatform.intel.com/shared_media/publication-optimize-pi0.5-paper.pdf)
- Intel's measured optimisation progress (Fig. 5, values read from the figure; 3 cams, 64 tokens, 10 steps, FP16): **555 → 365 → 294 → 234 → 213 → 172 ms** across "Cycle 1–5". The text attributes the cycles to OV IR, INT8 weight-only (">30%"), batched SigLIP, GEMM memory layout, SDPA fusion and GatedMLP fusion, but does not map each cycle to a step. The 90% LIBERO figure is for the FP16 OV model — [Intel π0.5 paper §4](https://docs.openedgeplatform.intel.com/shared_media/publication-optimize-pi0.5-paper.pdf)
- INT4 on the backbone: the earlier report's sources stand, namely QuantVLA W4A8 at 97.6% LIBERO and generic full-stack GPTQ/AWQ collapsing to 11–16%. The new evidence is Nota's INT4-sym MLP + INT8-sym on the **AE**, which kept LIBERO at 96.5%, and NNCF 2026.2's stated "INT4/INT8 weight compression support for VLA with Pi0.5" — [Nota AE-NPU card](https://huggingface.co/wjwjwjwj/nota-pi05-libero-ae-npu-openvino); [OpenVINO release notes](https://docs.openvino.ai/releasenotes)

### Inferences
- Our numbers: the prefix is about 296 ms in PyTorch-XPU (968 tokens). Nota's FP16 OV iGPU base at 10 steps is 250 ms total on the same GPU class. The difference between their 10-step and 1-step means (258 vs 175 ms) implies about **9 ms per denoise step** in OV against our 17 ms in PyTorch, and an OV FP16 prefix of roughly 150–165 ms. These are derived figures, not measurements.
- Our expected stack on the 388H: OV 2026.4 FP16 monolithic at 10 steps ≈ 250 ms. INT8_SYM weights should give roughly −20–30% on the prefix (Intel ">30%"; Fig. 4 W8A16/W8A8). SnapFlow at 1 step removes about 80 ms. `tokenizer_max_length` 200 → 64 removes about 14% of prefix tokens. Together these should land around **90–130 ms per chunk** with 3 cameras. That is an inference that needs measuring.
- Dropping `inspect` (3 → 2 cameras) or VTP-style pruning is the next lever. Both change the policy's inputs and need retraining or re-evaluation on our task, and VTP needs a trained sampler. Lowering resolution below 224 changes the SigLIP token grid (pos-embedding interpolation) and has no π0.5 evidence, so I found nothing to support it.

### Gaps
- No SO-101 real-robot accuracy for SnapFlow or 1-step π0.5. No published Panther Lake numbers for INT4-backbone π0.5 or for 2-camera π0.5.

## 6. Newer (mid/late 2026) published VLA latencies on Panther Lake / Lunar Lake with OpenVINO

### Takeaway
The only new third-party Panther Lake π0.5 numbers are Nota AI's Hugging Face cards from Q4 2026. iGPU-only OV 2026.4 FP16 gave **250 ms** (10-step, 968 tokens), about **175 ms mean** (1-step) and **105 ms median** (1-step + 50% visual-token pruning). Intel's 172 ms (64 tokens, INT8, debug-only fusions) is still the only vendor figure. I found no Lunar Lake OpenVINO π0.5 number and no SmolVLA OpenVINO number on Core Ultra 3.

### Cited Findings
- Nota AI's figures (X7 358H, OV 2026.4) are in section 4/5 — [HF nota-pi05-libero-vtp-drift-openvino](https://huggingface.co/geonmin-kim/nota-pi05-libero-vtp-drift-openvino); [HF nota-pi05-libero-ae-npu-openvino](https://huggingface.co/wjwjwjwj/nota-pi05-libero-ae-npu-openvino)
- vla.cpp (a SYCL/ggml fork, **not OpenVINO**): SmolVLA on a Core Ultra 7 356H, 2 views at 512 px, 121–131 ms fused against 558 ms unoptimised. It also says a SigLIP vision encoder ported to OV runs at 31 ms per view on the NPU (search-result summary, PR not opened) — [VinRobotics/vla.cpp PR #73](https://github.com/VinRobotics/vla.cpp/pull/73)
- Intel's PyTorch 2.12 XPU number for π0.5 on PTL at 40 W is 294 ms (vs Thor 287 ms) — [OEP article](https://docs.openedgeplatform.intel.com/dev/OEP-articles/publications/optimizing-pi0.5-lva-model.html)

### Inferences
- Our PyTorch-XPU 467 ms at 10 steps is about 1.6× slower than Intel's 294 ms PyTorch 2.12 number. The likely causes are 200 vs 64 language tokens, bf16 vs FP16, and the PyTorch version or power mode. Moving to OV 2026.4 FP16 alone should bring us near Nota's 250 ms.

### Gaps
- There are no independent reproductions of Intel's 172 ms. Nota's latencies come from single-device, single-episode Windows measurements. I found no Lunar Lake or Arc B-series π0.5 OpenVINO numbers.
